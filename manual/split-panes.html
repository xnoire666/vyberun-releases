Manual › The terminal

Split panes

Several terminals in one tab, or several tabs side by side.

Splitting a tab

KeysSplits
Ctrl+Shift+DTo the right
Ctrl+Shift+SDownward
Ctrl+Shift+ATo the left
Ctrl+Shift+WUpward

The new pane opens in the same folder, and it's the one you type in. In a server tab, it opens a new connection to the same server, in the folder you were in. Terminal ▸ Split offers the same.

Tip. If your typing suddenly goes somewhere else, a split may have opened by accident. Close it with Ctrl+Shift+Q.

Working with panes

  • Move between panes: Alt and the arrow keys. A coloured outline shows the pane you're in.
  • Zoom a pane to fill the tab: Ctrl+Shift+Z. Press it again to see them all.
  • Close a pane: Ctrl+Shift+Q. The others take its room.
  • Resize: drag the line between two panes.

Settings ▸ Terminal ▸ Split panes ▸ Dim unfocused panes makes the panes you're not typing in dimmer.

Moving a pane elsewhere

Hold Ctrl and right-click the pane ▸ Move:

  • Into tab puts it in another tab.
  • To project sends it to another card, still running.

Tab groups

A group shows several tabs side by side, and stands for them in the tab row as one tab. Each tab stays as it is: its panes, its name and what runs in it.

  1. Hold Ctrl and click the tabs you want. The one you're on counts too.
  2. Press Ctrl+Shift+G. They fold into one group tab and show side by side.
  3. Click a tile's bar to type in it. Ctrl+Shift+Z makes that tile fill the view, and again shows them all.
  • A tile's ✕ takes that tab out of the group. It keeps running.
  • Double-click the group tab to rename it. Right-click it for Color and Ungroup.
  • Click another tab and the group waits as its tab. Groups stay after a restart.

A group holds up to 6 tabs. Change it in Settings ▸ Terminal ▸ Tab groups (2 to 9). Above 6, each terminal gets small: best on a large or second monitor.
